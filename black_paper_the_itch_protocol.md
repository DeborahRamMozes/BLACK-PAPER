# THE ITCH PROTOCOL
## A Blackpaper on Craving, Aversive Prediction Error, Reward Devaluation, and Memory Reconsolidation

**Deborah Ram Mozes**  
**2026**

---

## ABSTRACT

Craving is commonly discussed as if it were a direct chemical command generated by the brain: dopamine rises, desire appears, the organism obeys. This explanation is useful but incomplete.

Human craving is also anticipatory.

A person does not need to consume chocolate before wanting chocolate. A smell can be enough. A wrapper can be enough. A room, a person, a certain hour, stress, loneliness, music, bodily tension, or memory can be enough.

The object may not even be physically present.

The brain predicts it.

This blackpaper begins from a simple speculative question:

**If craving depends partly upon a stored prediction that an object will produce a desirable experience, what happens when the predicted desirable outcome is replaced by a sufficiently convincing negative or worthless outcome?**

A person desires chocolate.

They taste it.

Instead of the anticipated pleasure, the experience is unexpectedly awful.

The brain receives not merely an unpleasant sensation but a contradiction:

> What I expected to be rewarding was not rewarding.

The same logic raises a more difficult question concerning addictive substances.

If cues associated with a substance activate memories, expectations, bodily anticipation, and incentive value before consumption occurs, could those networks be updated so that the substance remains recognizable but loses its predicted value?

The central proposition of **The Itch Protocol** is therefore not that memory should be erased.

Nor that the desired object must become terrifying.

Nor that dopamine must somehow be eliminated.

The proposition is narrower:

> **A craving-linked memory may become less motivationally powerful if the anticipated reward attached to that memory is successfully and durably devalued.**

The desired endpoint is not necessarily hatred.

It is indifference.

---

# 1. THE ITCH

The thought began with an ordinary observation.

Humans crave things they remember liking.

Chocolate.

Coffee.

A cigarette.

A particular food.

A particular person.

A particular sensation.

A drug.

The actual object is only one part of the system.

Around the object exists an architecture of association:

```text
PERSON
PLACE
SMELL
TASTE
TIME
STRESS
BODY STATE
MEMORY
RITUAL
     ↓
ASSOCIATIVE NETWORK
     ↓
EXPECTED OUTCOME
     ↓
REWARD VALUE
     ↓
CRAVING
```

The craving therefore begins before consumption.

The brain is not merely responding.

It is forecasting.

The question becomes:

> What happens if the forecast becomes unreliable?

---

# 2. CRAVING AS PREDICTION

Consider chocolate.

Before chocolate enters the mouth, the brain may already predict:

```text
sweetness
texture
smell
pleasure
comfort
reward
```

The cue itself can therefore acquire motivational value.

```text
CHOCOLATE
     ↓
MEMORY OF CHOCOLATE
     ↓
EXPECTED PLEASURE
     ↓
WANTING
```

The crucial object is not simply chocolate.

It is:

**CHOCOLATE-AS-PREDICTED-REWARD.**

Now introduce an unexpected outcome.

The person strongly expects delicious chocolate.

Instead:

```text
CHOCOLATE
     ↓
EXPECTED PLEASURE
     ↓
ACTUAL EXPERIENCE = AWFUL
```

Something cognitively important has occurred.

The brain predicted one state.

Reality delivered another.

That discrepancy is a **prediction error**.

---

# 3. THE NEGATIVE PREDICTION ERROR

A simple conceptual representation:

```text
EXPECTED VALUE = +8

EXPERIENCED VALUE = -4

PREDICTION ERROR = -12
```

The numbers are illustrative, not biological measurements.

The significance lies in the direction and magnitude of the contradiction.

The organism predicted:

> GOOD.

The organism received:

> NOT GOOD.

A learning system that refused to update after such contradictions would be useless.

Animals survive because predictions can change.

Food associated with sickness becomes suspicious.

Places associated with danger become avoided.

Objects associated with reward become approached.

Value is learned.

Therefore value can also be revised.

---

# 4. CONDITIONED TASTE AVERSION

One of the clearest demonstrations comes from conditioned taste aversion.

An organism consumes a particular flavor.

Later it experiences nausea or illness.

The brain may rapidly establish:

```text
THAT TASTE
     =
BAD CONSEQUENCE
```

Remarkably, this association can sometimes develop after very limited exposure.

Evolution has an obvious reason for preserving such learning.

An organism that repeatedly eats poisonous berries because it refuses to update its culinary opinion has a limited reproductive career.

The brain therefore possesses mechanisms capable of dramatically revising the value assigned to previously acceptable stimuli.

The Itch begins here but does not end here.

---

# 5. LIKING IS NOT WANTING

A major complication appears immediately.

**Liking and wanting are not identical.**

A person may enjoy something without compulsively pursuing it.

A person may also compulsively pursue something they barely enjoy anymore.

This distinction becomes particularly important in addiction.

A person may say:

> I hate what this substance does to me.

And nevertheless crave it.

Therefore:

```text
DISLIKE ≠ ZERO CRAVING
```

Likewise:

```text
SUFFERING ≠ AUTOMATIC DEVALUATION
```

This is why simply making something taste disgusting cannot be assumed to eliminate addiction.

The brain is irritatingly more complicated than a restaurant review.

---

# 6. INCENTIVE SALIENCE

A substance-related cue can acquire motivational force.

A room.

A lighter.

A syringe.

A smell.

A person.

Money.

A street.

A certain emotional state.

These cues can become loaded with **incentive salience**.

They do not merely mean:

> I remember the substance.

They may function more like:

> Something important is available. Go get it.

This distinction changes the research target.

The objective is not merely to generate dislike.

The deeper objective becomes:

> Reduce the motivational significance assigned to the cue.

---

# 7. MEMORY IS NOT A STATIC FILE

The everyday metaphor of memory as a permanent stored recording is misleading.

Memory behaves less like a sealed archive and more like a reconstructed network.

When a memory is retrieved, under certain conditions it may become temporarily unstable.

Before becoming stable again, aspects of that memory may be modified.

This process is called:

## MEMORY RECONSOLIDATION

Conceptually:

```text
STORED MEMORY
     ↓
RETRIEVAL
     ↓
TEMPORARY LABILE STATE
     ↓
NEW INFORMATION
     ↓
RESTABILIZATION
```

The important word is:

**UPDATE.**

Not delete.

Not erase.

Update.

---

# 8. THE CENTRAL COLLISION

Now combine reward prediction with reconsolidation.

A cue activates an old reward memory.

The organism predicts the familiar reward.

Instead, something substantially different occurs.

```text
CUE
 ↓
MEMORY RETRIEVAL
 ↓
EXPECTED REWARD
 ↓
UNEXPECTED OUTCOME
 ↓
PREDICTION ERROR
 ↓
MEMORY UPDATE
```

The Itch asks:

> Could the value attached to the retrieved memory be reduced during this state?

If yes, then the organism may retain:

```text
WHAT THE OBJECT IS
```

while losing part of:

```text
WHY THE OBJECT MUST BE PURSUED
```

That distinction is the core of this blackpaper.

---

# 9. THE CHOCOLATE MODEL

### Original network

```text
SEE CHOCOLATE
      ↓
REMEMBER CHOCOLATE
      ↓
ANTICIPATE DELICIOUS TASTE
      ↓
EXPECTED REWARD
      ↓
DESIRE
      ↓
CONSUMPTION
```

### Prediction violation

```text
SEE CHOCOLATE
      ↓
STRONG EXPECTATION
      ↓
CONSUME
      ↓
UNEXPECTEDLY AWFUL EXPERIENCE
      ↓
NEGATIVE PREDICTION ERROR
```

### Possible updated network

```text
SEE CHOCOLATE
      ↓
RETRIEVE UPDATED VALUE
      ↓
EXPECTED REWARD LOWER
      ↓
DESIRE LOWER
```

The theoretical endpoint is not:

```text
CHOCOLATE = HORROR
```

It is:

```text
CHOCOLATE = NOT WORTH MUCH
```

---

# 10. WHY INDIFFERENCE MAY BE MORE IMPORTANT THAN DISGUST

Disgust is still attention.

Fear is still attention.

Hatred is still attention.

Trauma can make an object extremely salient.

The emotional sign changes, but the object remains neurologically important.

Therefore an intervention producing:

```text
LOVE → TERROR
```

may simply exchange one high-salience relationship for another.

The more elegant transition is:

```text
HIGH VALUE
     ↓
LOW VALUE
     ↓
IRRELEVANCE
```

The object remains identifiable.

It simply stops shouting.

---

# 11. THE DRUG PROBLEM

The naive translation would be:

```text
MAKE DRUG EXPERIENCE AWFUL
     ↓
BRAIN LEARNS DRUG IS BAD
     ↓
CRAVING DISAPPEARS
```

Reality refuses to be this courteous.

People with substance-use disorders frequently continue using despite experiences that are already profoundly unpleasant:

```text
nausea
withdrawal
panic
financial loss
relationship damage
medical consequences
shame
danger
```

Therefore repeated negative consequences alone are insufficient.

The addiction network may contain:

```text
pharmacological reinforcement
habit
withdrawal relief
stress regulation
incentive salience
contextual memory
social cues
interoceptive cues
automatic behavior
reward prediction
```

Making one sensory property unpleasant may leave the rest intact.

---

# 12. AVERSION-RESISTANT REWARD

Animal experiments provide an especially important warning.

When rewarding substances are paired with an unpleasant adulterant such as quinine, some animals reduce consumption.

Others continue.

Some even maintain substantial intake despite the aversive taste.

This reveals two partially independent computations:

```text
THIS TASTES BAD
```

and:

```text
I STILL WANT THE REWARD
```

The existence of aversion-resistant consumption means that disgust alone cannot be treated as a universal cure.

The relevant question becomes:

> Can the anticipated reward itself be rewritten?

---

# 13. AVERSIVE COUNTERCONDITIONING DURING RECONSOLIDATION

Preclinical research has produced evidence particularly close to the Itch hypothesis.

Drug-associated memories can be retrieved.

Aversive counterconditioning can then be introduced during a period when those memories may be modifiable.

Experiments involving cocaine- and morphine-associated memories in rodents have reported reductions in later drug-seeking when aversive counterconditioning occurred shortly after retrieval.

Critically, timing mattered.

The same procedure delivered substantially later did not produce equivalent effects.

This matters because it suggests something stronger than ordinary competing learning may be occurring.

The original memory may be undergoing modification.

# 15. APER

## AVERSIVE PREDICTION-ERROR RECONSOLIDATION

For this blackpaper, the speculative mechanism is termed:

# APER

APER does not mean maximizing unpleasantness.

It means introducing a sufficiently meaningful contradiction between:

```text
EXPECTED REWARD
```

and:

```text
RECEIVED OR REPRESENTED OUTCOME
```

while the relevant memory network is in a state capable of updating.

Conceptually:

```text
R = retrieval of reward memory

P = predicted reward

O = unexpected outcome

PE = prediction error

M = memory modifiability

V = updated incentive value

C = subsequent craving
```

The speculative relationship is:

```text
R
+
SUFFICIENT PE
+
SUFFICIENT M
→
V ↓
→
C ↓
```

This is not presented as a biological equation.

It is a research architecture.

---

# 16. WHY THE BRAIN MUST BELIEVE THE ERROR

An arbitrary unpleasant stimulus may not be enough.

Learning requires relevance.

Suppose someone expects chocolate and, immediately afterward, hears an irritating alarm.

The brain may learn:

```text
CHOCOLATE + ALARM
```

rather than:

```text
CHOCOLATE IS LESS REWARDING THAN EXPECTED
```

For true value updating, the contradictory experience may need to violate the prediction that generated the craving.

The error must belong to the same model.

```text
EXPECTED TASTE
vs.
ACTUAL TASTE

EXPECTED RELIEF
vs.
NO RELIEF

EXPECTED PLEASURE
vs.
NO PLEASURE

EXPECTED REWARD
vs.
VALUELESS OUTCOME
```

The closer the contradiction attacks the prediction itself, the more theoretically interesting the update becomes.

---

# 17. IMAGINED EXPERIENCE

Another strange opening appears.

The brain responds not only to physical experience.

Mental simulation can influence reward processing.

Repeatedly imagining consumption of a food has been shown experimentally to reduce subsequent consumption under certain conditions.

This does not prove that imagination can erase addiction.

It demonstrates something narrower and more useful:

> Internally generated sensory representation can modify subsequent reward-related behavior.

The boundary between:

```text
REAL EXPERIENCE
```

and:

```text
SIMULATED EXPERIENCE
```

is therefore not absolute at the level of valuation.

This creates a major theoretical possibility.

The contradiction necessary for reward devaluation may not always require physically consuming the desired object.

---

# 18. THE INTERNAL REWARD SIMULATOR

Craving frequently occurs without the object being present.

Therefore part of craving must involve internal simulation.

```text
CUE
 ↓
MENTAL REPRESENTATION
 ↓
PREDICTED SENSORY STATE
 ↓
PREDICTED BODILY STATE
 ↓
EXPECTED REWARD
 ↓
CRAVING
```

The brain is effectively rendering a future.

The Itch asks whether that rendering engine itself can be manipulated.

Not by lying consciously:

> This is bad.

But by altering the internally predicted experience strongly enough that the valuation system updates.

The radical question becomes:

> **Can the brain be made to experience a sufficiently credible counterfactual outcome that it recalculates what the object is worth before reality occurs?**

---

# 19. TRAUMA IS NOT THE TARGET

A powerful negative memory might appear attractive because humans can acquire persistent fear after severe experiences.

But trauma is a poor model for therapeutic devaluation.

Trauma can create:

```text
hypervigilance
stress
intrusive recollection
avoidance
physiological arousal
generalized fear
```

Stress itself can increase vulnerability to relapse.

Therefore:

```text
STRONGER SUFFERING
≠
BETTER DEVALUATION
```

The useful target is:

```text
MAXIMUM INFORMATIONAL CONTRADICTION
WITH
MINIMUM HARM
```

Not terror.

Not poisoning.

Not humiliation.

Not traumatic conditioning.

---

# 20. THE MEMORY DOES NOT NEED TO DISAPPEAR

A common fantasy surrounding addiction treatment is complete erasure.

Erase the memory.

Erase desire.

Erase dopamine response.

Erase the drug from the psychological map.

But memory deletion may not be necessary.

The person can remember:

```text
the substance
the experience
the environment
the past
```

without assigning those memories high motivational value.

The target can therefore be narrower:

```text
IDENTITY OF MEMORY = PRESERVED

INCENTIVE VALUE OF MEMORY = REDUCED
```

This is arguably a more realistic architecture than memory erasure.

---

# 21. CRAVING ZERO

The original itch imagined craving reaching zero.

Scientifically, absolute zero may be the wrong conceptual target.

Craving fluctuates.

Context changes.

Stress changes.

Memory changes.

Physiology changes.

Instead of:

```text
CRAVING = 0 FOREVER
```

a more meaningful outcome would be:

```text
CUE NO LONGER AUTOMATICALLY PRODUCES
HIGH MOTIVATIONAL PRIORITY
```

The difference is important.

The first demands neurological perfection.

The second demands successful revaluation.

---

# 22. NETWORK DEVALUATION

One difficulty remains.

A drug memory is unlikely to consist of a single association.

It may resemble a network:

```text
               SMELL
                 │
                 │
PLACE ──────── DRUG ─────── PERSON
                 │
                 │
              RELIEF
                 │
              STRESS
                 │
              RITUAL
```

Changing only taste may leave:

```text
PLACE → CRAVING

STRESS → CRAVING

PERSON → CRAVING
```

untouched.

Therefore the deeper form of the Itch concerns:

# NETWORK DEVALUATION

Can multiple predictive pathways pointing toward the same reward be updated so that the entire network loses incentive strength?

This is substantially more difficult than producing one aversion.

It may also be the actual problem.

---

# 23. REWARD WITHOUT REWARD

Another possible state deserves attention.

Suppose the substance remains pharmacologically active.

The user still experiences physiological effects.

Yet the learned anticipatory value attached to the ritual and cues declines.

This would produce an unusual dissociation:

```text
PHARMACOLOGICAL EFFECT
REMAINS

ANTICIPATORY INCENTIVE VALUE
DECLINES
```

Such a state would help separate two things frequently collapsed into one word:

**drug effect**

and

**drug craving.**

The substance can chemically act on the organism while becoming psychologically less worth pursuing.

---

# 24. THE BLACKPAPER HYPOTHESIS

The Itch Protocol proposes:

> **Craving is partly maintained by learned predictions assigning high incentive value to objects and their associated cues. If a relevant reward memory is retrieved under conditions permitting modification, and its predicted outcome is contradicted by a sufficiently credible and salient negative or null outcome, the incentive value attached to that memory may be reduced during reconsolidation.**

Further:

> **The ideal endpoint is not necessarily conditioned fear or disgust, but preserved recognition accompanied by reduced predicted reward and reduced motivational salience.**

In compressed form:

```text
MEMORY
+
PREDICTION
+
VALUE
=
CRAVING POTENTIAL
```

Therefore:

```text
ALTER VALUE
WITHOUT ERASING MEMORY
=
POSSIBLE CRAVING REDUCTION
```

---

# 25. FALSIFIABILITY

The Itch Protocol fails if controlled research demonstrates that prediction-error interventions consistently produce only temporary conscious dislike while leaving incentive behavior unchanged.

It also fails if:

```text
retrieval does not enhance updating,

reward predictions rapidly return unchanged,

cue-triggered behavior remains intact,

negative conditioning increases rather than decreases salience,

or apparent effects are merely compliance with experimental instructions.
```

A theory that cannot lose is not a theory.

It is decoration.

---

# 26. EVIDENCE BOUNDARY

### Supported scientific foundations

Reward value can change.

Cue associations influence craving.

Wanting and liking can dissociate.

Conditioned taste aversion can strongly modify preference.

Prediction error contributes to learning.

Retrieved memories can under some circumstances undergo reconsolidation.

Imagined consumption can influence subsequent consumption.

Aversive counterconditioning following memory retrieval has altered later drug-seeking in preclinical models.

### Not established

That human addiction can be permanently eliminated using this mechanism.

That one aversive experience will reliably abolish drug craving.

That imagination alone can reliably devalue addictive substances.

That a universal reconsolidation window exists.

That all cue networks can be simultaneously rewritten.

That craving can be reduced permanently to absolute zero.

The boundary matters.

Speculation becomes research only when it remembers which side of the evidence it is standing on.

---

# 27. ETHICAL BOUNDARY

This blackpaper does not propose:

```text
poisoning
drug adulteration
forced consumption
induced vomiting
traumatic conditioning
coercion
uncontrolled exposure to illicit substances
```

The scientific question concerns **memory and valuation**, not how brutally one can manufacture suffering.

An intervention that replaces addiction with another psychological injury has solved nothing.

---

# 28. THE DEEPER QUESTION

The chocolate thought experiment exposes something larger than food.

Humans continuously act toward futures that have not happened.

They pursue an internal model.

```text
I REMEMBER IT.
        ↓
I PREDICT IT.
        ↓
I VALUE THAT PREDICTION.
        ↓
I WANT IT.
```

Therefore craving may be understood partly as:

# A MEMORY OF A FUTURE REWARD.

That phrase contains the paradox.

The reward has not happened.

But the remembered model of previous reward generates a future expectation.

The organism then behaves toward the expectation.

Change the expectation and behavior may change before the object arrives.

---

# 29. FINAL FORM

The Itch is not:

> How can the brain be forced to hate chocolate?

It is not:

> How can dopamine be removed?

It is not:

> How can an addicted person be traumatized into stopping?

It is:

> **How can the predicted value encoded around a desired object be made sufficiently wrong that the brain stops treating the object as worth pursuing?**

The desired transformation is:

```text
CUE
 ↓
MEMORY
 ↓
EXPECTED REWARD
 ↓
CRAVING
```

into:

```text
CUE
 ↓
MEMORY
 ↓
LOW OR NULL EXPECTED VALUE
 ↓
INDIFFERENCE
```

The object remains.

The memory remains.

The person remains.

What changes is the promise.

---

# THE ITCH PROTOCOL

```text
DO NOT ERASE THE OBJECT.

DO NOT ERASE THE MEMORY.

DO NOT REPLACE DESIRE WITH TRAUMA.

BREAK THE PROMISE OF REWARD.

LET THE BRAIN REMEMBER
WITHOUT NEEDING TO WANT.
```
